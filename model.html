Model story

A story about each model.

Expand a model to see a glimpse of a story.

Ford

Model T
In 1908, Henry Ford didn't just build a car; he built a new way of life. The "Tin Lizzie" was the first vehicle made on a moving assembly line, making car ownership possible for the average person and effectively "putting the world on wheels."
Mustang
Launched at the 1964 World's Fair, the Mustang created the "Pony Car" class. It was designed to be a stylish, affordable sports car for the youth, selling over 400,000 units in its first year and becoming an instant American pop-culture icon.
Super Duty
Introduced in 1998 to separate heavy-duty work from daily driving, the Super Duty became the backbone of industry. It’s a specialized beast built for towing and hauling, proving that a truck can be a sophisticated office and a powerhouse at the same time.

Toyota

Corolla
Debuting in 1966, the Corolla was built with the simple goal of being "the most wanted car by the market." By 1997, it became the best-selling nameplate in history, known globally as the gold standard for reliability and practical transportation.
Prius
When it arrived in 1997, the Prius was a radical experiment. As the first mass-produced hybrid, it survived early skepticism to change how the world thinks about fuel efficiency, paving the way for the entire modern electric movement.

Volkswagen

Type 2
Born in the 1950s from a simple sketch of a parts-hauler, the "Bus" became the ultimate symbol of counter-culture and freedom. Its boxy, friendly face defined the "hippie" era and the spirit of the nomadic road trip.
ID. Buzz
For 2026, the legend returns without the engine noise. The ID. Buzz translates the classic Microbus silhouette into a high tech electric van, proving that the future of family travel can still have a retro soul.

Mercedes-Benz

300SL
Originally a 1950s racing car, the "Gullwing" became the fastest production car of its time. Its roof-hinged doors weren't just for style—they were a technical necessity for its unique space-frame chassis, creating a masterpiece of form and function.
EQS Sedan
The 2026 EQS is the electric heir to the S-Class throne. With its massive "Hyperscreen" dashboard and whisper-quiet cabin, it represents the brand's shift from mechanical perfection to a digital-first luxury sanctuary.

Cadillac

Series 62
In the 1940s and 50s, the Series 62 defined the "Standard of the World." Its massive tailfins and heavy chrome were inspired by fighter jets, representing the peak of American optimism and mid-century architectural design.
CELESTIQ
he 2026 CELESTIQ is Cadillac’s return to hand-built exclusivity. Each car is a bespoke electric commission costing over $400,000, designed to compete with the finest European coachbuilders using ultra-modern tech.

Alfa Romeo

Spider Duetto
Made famous by the 1967 film The Graduate, the Duetto is the essence of Italian romanticism. Its "cuttlefish-bone" shape and twin-cam engine made it a car meant for winding coastal roads and sunset drives.
Giulia Quadrifoglio
For 2026, the Giulia remains the "soul" of the brand. With a Ferrari-derived V6 and perfect 50/50 balance, it’s a luxury sedan that behaves like a supercar, keeping the brand's racing heart beating in the modern day.

Tesla

Roadster
Tesla’s first act was a tiny, carbon-fiber sports car that proved electric cars didn't have to be boring golf carts. It was the spark that forced the entire automotive industry to take battery power seriously.
Model S Plaid
The 2026 Plaid is a masterclass in "sleeper" performance. It looks like a standard luxury sedan but possesses sub-2-second acceleration that can outpace almost any multi-million dollar hypercar on the planet.

GMC

Carryall-Suburban
Launched in 1935, this was the world’s first SUV. It combined a truck chassis with a station wagon body, creating a "carryall" that could transport eight people—a formula that still dominates the roads today.
Sierra EV
The 2026 Sierra EV Denali Edition 1 is a "super-truck." With 754 horsepower and a 440-mile range, it’s a high-luxury electric powerhouse that can power your entire home during a blackout.

Rolls-Royce

Silver Ghost
In 1907, the Silver Ghost earned the title "The Best Car in the World" after driving 15,000 miles without a single mechanical failure. It established the brand’s reputation for peerless reliability and "waftability."
Spectre
The 2026 Spectre is the first electric Rolls-Royce. It is designed to be so silent and smooth that it feels less like a car and more like a private jet on wheels, marking the start of a fully electric era for the marque.

Ferrari

250 GTO
Often called the "Mona Lisa of Cars," the 250 GTO was built for racing but became a work of art. Only 36 were ever made, and today they are the most valuable collector items in the automotive world.
849 Testarossa
Unveiled for 2026, the 849 brings back a legendary name as a 1,050-horsepower hybrid. It blends the heritage of the 1970s "Testarossa" with a mid-rear V8, pushing the limits of plug-in hybrid performance.

Lamborghini

Miura
In 1966, the Miura shocked the world by putting the engine behind the driver, creating the "Supercar" blueprint. Its eyelashes and low-slung body made it look more like a predator than a vehicle.
Revuelto
The 2026 Revuelto is a "High Performance Electrified Vehicle." It pairs a screaming naturally aspirated V12 with three electric motors, ensuring that Lamborghini's future is both sustainable and incredibly loud.
